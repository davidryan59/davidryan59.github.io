The Spectre

The Spectre is a single tile that covers the whole plane with no gaps and no overlaps. The pattern it makes never repeats. David Smith, Joseph Samuel Myers, Craig S. Kaplan and Chaim Goodman-Strauss announced it in 2023. It settles the "einstein" problem in its strictest form: with curved edges, not even the tile's mirror image can join in.

The tile appears in 12 orientations. Six are common and sit 60° apart. The other six are rare. Each rare tile is turned a further 30°, and it pairs with one neighbour to make a two-tile cluster called a Mystic. In the default colours, the hue turns with the tile, so each colour marks one orientation.

With straight edges the tile is called Tile(1,1). It makes this pattern only if mirror images are banned, because with mirror images it can also tile periodically. Swap every edge for the same S-shaped curve and the mirror image no longer fits, so the ban is no longer needed. Any smooth S-curve works, which makes the Spectre a family of tiles rather than one shape. Turn on Curved edges and move the Bend slider to travel through part of that family.

Tiles group into supertiles, and those group into larger supertiles, level after level. Turn on Supertile outlines to see five levels of that hierarchy, drawn thicker as the level rises. This page builds each patch of tiles from the hierarchy as you move, so you never reach an edge.

Drag to move, and scroll, pinch or double-click to zoom. The arrow keys move, and + and − zoom. The address bar keeps your place, so you can share a view.
