Aperiodic pairs

Six tilings built from two tiles, each drawn here from its mathematical definition

An aperiodic set of tiles covers the plane, but never in a repeating pattern. For almost fifty years, the smallest known sets had two tiles, until the Hat and the Spectre showed in 2023 that one unmarked shape is enough. Here are six two-tile systems. Every picture is computed from the published definition, not copied from a paper.

Drag a picture to move it. Pinch, Ctrl+scroll or the buttons zoom it; on a phone, use two fingers.

Kites and darts Proved

Roger Penrose, 1974

kite dart

A kite has angles of 72°, 72°, 72° and 144°. A dart has angles of 36°, 72°, 36° and 216°. Both have edges of length φ and 1, where φ = (1 + √5)/2 is the golden ratio. The bare shapes also tile periodically, so the pair needs matching rules: marked corners, arcs or bumps. The patch starts from a sun of five kites. Seven rounds of subdivision split every half-kite and half-dart into smaller ones, cutting at the golden ratio.

Penrose rhombs Proved

Roger Penrose, 1974

thick rhomb thin rhomb

A thick rhomb with angles of 72° and 108°, and a thin rhomb with angles of 36° and 144°, all edges equal. Like the kites and darts, they need matching rules. The patch starts from a wheel of ten half-rhombs and goes through seven rounds of subdivision. Half-rhombs on the rim show as triangles.

Inflationφ ≈ 1.618

Ammann A2 Proved

Robert Ammann, 1977; published by Grünbaum and Shephard, 1987

big tile small tile

Two L-shaped hexagons with only right angles. The small tile is the big one scaled by Ψ = 1/√φ ≈ 0.786. Going clockwise from the longest side, the big tile's sides are 1, Ψ, Ψ², Ψ⁵, Ψ⁴ and Ψ³. Each round of subdivision turns a big tile into a big and a small tile, and a small tile into a big one, all scaled by Ψ. So the tile counts follow the Fibonacci numbers. Marks on the edges enforce the hierarchy; they are not drawn here.

Tiles in the patch4,181 after 17 rounds
Inflation√φ ≈ 1.272

Trilobite and crab Proved

Chaim Goodman-Strauss, 1999 and 2016

Chair tiling, coloured by the four turns

The trilobite and crab live on a square grid. In the encoding Goodman-Strauss uses for his proof, every cell holds one tile, every tile is notched at its four corners, and a trilobite also carries tips at two opposite corners. Every corner of the grid must take exactly one tip. The pair descends from his trilobite and cross of 1999, whose tilings are recompositions of chair tilings: every chair contributes one trilobite. The patch shows that chair hierarchy, after five rounds of the chair substitution. The tiles' curved markings carry further rules and are not drawn here.

Hierarchychair, inflation 2
Orientations used8 in all

Taylor–Socolar hexagon Proved

Joan Taylor and Joshua Socolar, 2010

tile mirror image diameters

A hexagon and its mirror image, marked with black stripes and with red and blue diameters. Rule R1: black stripes continue across every edge. Rule R2: at the two ends of any edge, the diameters that line up with it have different colours. Those diameters belong to the tiles just past each end, so R2 links tiles that do not touch. With reflections allowed, the pair is a single tile; without them, it is an aperiodic pair. A SAT solver found this patch of 469 hexagons from the two rules alone. The same encoding admits no repeating pattern on any torus up to 16 by 32 hexagons, as the theorem requires.

Hierarchyhalf-hex, inflation 2
Orderlimit-periodic

A two-shape Spectre tiling Tilings

Family: Michael Baake, Franz Gähler, Jan Mazáč and Lorenzo Sadun, 2025

even turns odd turns

The Spectre is one tile with fourteen equal edges, pointing in the twelve directions at multiples of 30°. Stretch a Spectre tiling: give the six directions at even multiples of 30° length a, and the other six length b. With a = b, this is the Spectre tiling. Otherwise, about one tile in 8.9 turns by an odd multiple of 30°, which swaps its edge lengths and makes it a second shape. The tiling keeps the Spectre's hierarchy, but nobody claims the two shapes force aperiodicity on their own. The slider stretches a level-4 supertile of 4,401 tiles.
