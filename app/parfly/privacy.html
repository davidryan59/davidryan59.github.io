Parfly privacy policy

Version 1, effective 23 September 2026

Parfly records your journeys by GPS and keeps them on your phone. It has no account, no ads and no analytics. It never sends your rides to me or to anyone else. It goes online only when you look at a ride over a Google map.

Parfly is made by David Ryan, an independent developer. If you have a question about this policy, email me at parfly@drbuild.uk.

What Parfly records

Parfly records only between the moment you press Record and the moment you press Finish. Android shows a notification for the whole of that time.

Once a second during a ride, it stores:

  • your position, and how accurate the phone says it is
  • your altitude, and how accurate that is
  • your speed and your direction of travel
  • the time

For each ride it also stores:

  • the name you give the ride, its start and finish times, and the vehicle and phone mount you pick
  • any marks you press, each with its time, position, type and any note you add
  • when the signal dropped, when you paused, and when Parfly went into or out of the background, so that a gap in the record can be explained
  • the rider name you enter, and a random ID that tells your rides apart from another rider's when ride files are compared

Where it is kept

Everything stays in Parfly's own storage on your phone. So do your settings: your rider name, the map view you last picked, and whether diagnostics are shown. Parfly has no server, so nothing is uploaded and I never receive your rides.

If you have turned on Android's backup, Android may include Parfly's data in your phone's backup to your Google account. Google runs that backup under its own terms, and I cannot see it.

Permissions

  • Location, to record where you go. Parfly asks for it while the app is open. During a ride you started, it keeps recording when you switch to another app or turn the screen off.
  • Notifications, to show that a ride is recording.
  • Internet, only for the Google map views described next.

Google Maps

A finished ride can be shown in four views. Trace Only, the first time you open a ride, draws your route on a plain background and contacts nobody. Map, Satellite and Terrain draw it over a Google map.

When you pick one of those three, the Google Maps SDK loads the map from Google. Google then receives the map area you look at and how you pan and zoom, your phone's IP address, an identifier for the Maps SDK, and crash and performance data. Parfly does not give Google your ride: the route is drawn over the map on your phone. The map area you look at still shows roughly where you went.

Google's Privacy Policy covers what Google receives. Using those three views means you accept the Google Maps/Google Earth Additional Terms of Service.

When you share a ride

The Share button makes two files for a ride, a GPX file and a JSON file, and passes them to Android's share menu. They go only where you send them.

Look before you send one to someone you do not know. A ride file shows where you started and finished, which can reveal your home, and the time and speed of every part of the journey. The ride's name is in both files and in their filenames. The JSON file also carries your rider name and rider ID.

If you import a ride file, Parfly reads only the file you choose.

What Parfly does not do

  • It has no account and no sign-in.
  • It shows no ads.
  • It runs no analytics or tracking.
  • It does not sell or share your data. I never receive it.

Deleting your data

Delete a ride from Past rides. Uninstalling Parfly deletes everything it stored on your phone. Files you have already shared stay wherever you sent them.

Children

Parfly is made for adults and is not directed at children.

Your rights

Your rides never reach me, so I hold no personal data about you from the app. There is nothing for me to show you, correct or delete, and you can delete everything yourself as described above. If you email me, I use your address and your message only to reply to you.

Changes to this policy

If Parfly ever sends data anywhere new, this policy will change first, and the app will ask you before anything is sent. Each change gets a new version number and date at the top of this page.
