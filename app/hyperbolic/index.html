About this explorer

The hyperbolic plane is a surface where the angles of a triangle add up to less than 180°. It has more room than a flat plane, so seven triangles, or eight, fit round one corner. No flat map shows it without distortion, so mathematicians draw it in many ways. Each way, a model, keeps some properties true and gives up others.

Every model here shows the same tiling. Drag the plane, and the tiles move as one rigid motion of the hyperbolic plane. Switch the model, and the same tiles stay in the same place.

  • Poincaré disk, half-plane, band, Joukowsky, inverted disk: true angles, so every small tile keeps its shape.
  • Klein disk: straight lines stay straight.
  • Hyperboloid and Gans plane: the surface in 3D space that the other models come from, and its view from directly above.
  • The six map projections: the hyperbolic versions of map projections of the Earth, which keep distances or areas true.

The tilings come from triangles with angles 180°/p, 180°/q and 180°/r. Reflections in the sides of one triangle fill the whole plane. The tiles group the triangles round each corner of angle 180°/p.
